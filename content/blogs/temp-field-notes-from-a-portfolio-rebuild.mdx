A lot of people treat philosophy as decorative intelligence.

Interesting, maybe. Respectable, maybe. But not especially useful once the real work begins.

I think that is backwards.

Philosophy becomes more useful as systems become more powerful. Not less.

## The problem underneath the tools

Technology gives us expanded action. Philosophy asks whether the action is coherent.

Without that second layer, people end up with strange modern pathologies:

- they can build fast, but cannot choose cleanly
- they can optimize everything, but do not know what should remain sacred
- they can manufacture reach, but not necessarily meaning

## Why I keep returning to it

Philosophy trains a particular kind of seriousness.

It makes you inspect your assumptions. It forces you to define your terms. It asks what follows if what you say is actually true. That is useful in code, in design, in ambition, and in ordinary life.

The point is not to become abstract. The point is to become harder to fool, including by yourself.

## A practical definition

For me, philosophy is not mainly a body of historical texts.

It is the repeated act of asking:

- What is really happening here?
- What do I believe about it?
- What follows from that belief?
- What kind of person will this choice train me to become?

That seems practical enough to keep.
