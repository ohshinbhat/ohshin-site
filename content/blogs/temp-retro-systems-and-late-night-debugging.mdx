I have a bias toward interfaces that show a little of their machinery.

Not because I want software to feel old, but because I want it to feel truthful.

The smoothest product in the world can still feel brittle if the user cannot tell what state it is in, where data came from, or what will happen after they press the button. A little visible structure goes a long way.

## Trust is a design primitive

Trust does not come from polish alone. It comes from feedback.

Where am I in the flow?
What just changed?
Is this loading, saved, synced, or pending?
What can I undo?

These are not small questions. They determine whether the interface feels like a collaborator or a trap.

## Modern products hide too much by default

There is a fashionable belief that the best interface disappears. Sometimes that is true. But disappearance is only good when understanding survives it.

When abstraction becomes too aggressive, the user loses orientation. The product may look calm, but the interaction becomes nervous. People click twice. They refresh manually. They distrust the workflow without always knowing why.

Visible systems solve that. Not with noise, but with honest signal.

Progress indicators. Status text. Clear transitions. Evidence that the machine has heard you.
