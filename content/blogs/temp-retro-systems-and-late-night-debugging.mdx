Every useful system has a texture.

Sometimes that texture is obvious: phosphor green terminals, beige plastic, keys that sound like tiny doors closing. Sometimes it is hidden inside the design of the workflow itself, where constraints quietly decide what kind of thinking is possible.

## What the old machines knew

Old interfaces were not always better, but they were often clearer. You could feel the edges of the machine. You knew when you were asking too much from it.

That sharp edge made the next decision easier.

- reduce the command
- make the state visible
- keep the interaction honest
- avoid pretending the machine is magic

## The debugging ritual

Late night debugging has its own weather. The room gets quiet, the logs become strangely intimate, and the problem starts to reveal the shape of the system around it.

That is the part I like: the moment where the bug stops being an interruption and becomes a map.

The fix is rarely only the fix. It is usually also a note from the future about where the design was vague.
