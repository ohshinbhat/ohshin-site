People talk about taste in software as if it were a mystical trait.

I do not think it is mystical. I think it is disciplined perception.

Taste is the ability to notice what is off before the metrics can explain it. But noticing is not enough. In a product environment, taste only becomes useful when it can survive repetition.

## From instinct to standard

An isolated good decision is nice. A system that keeps producing good decisions is better.

That means taste has to become operational:

- what spacing feels calm here
- what motion feels intentional rather than ornamental
- what copy sounds confident without sounding cold
- what complexity deserves interface surface and what should stay hidden

If those choices stay private inside one person's head, the team does not have taste. It has dependency.

## The real discipline

The real discipline is turning subjective sharpness into shared defaults without flattening the original point of view.

This is where a lot of teams fail. They either avoid standards in the name of creativity, or they standardize so aggressively that all taste disappears.

The balance is harder and more interesting:

- enough system to move quickly
- enough judgment to keep the work alive

That balance is one of the deepest product skills there is.
