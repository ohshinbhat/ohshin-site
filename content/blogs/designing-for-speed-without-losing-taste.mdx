Speed usually fails long before engineering does.

The failure mode is vague taste, vague constraints, and vague decision ownership. Teams say they want velocity, but what they actually create is **constant re-litigation**:

- the design is almost right but not decided
- the interaction is almost done but not reduced
- the copy is almost final but still floating

When that happens, code becomes the place where uncertainty accumulates.

## What speed actually needs

Speed needs a clear aesthetic direction and a small set of decisions that stay decided. Good systems do three things:

1. They define what "good" looks like.
2. They narrow the number of valid choices.
3. They make the next move obvious.

That applies to interfaces, design systems, and team habits.

## The bar I care about

I want interfaces that feel authored. That does not mean ornamental. It means there is a point of view behind spacing, motion, typography, and copy.

The fastest teams I have seen are not the ones with the most process. They are the ones where intent is legible.

## Practical rule

If a section of the product keeps getting redesigned, the issue is usually not execution speed. The issue is that the team never agreed on the decision frame.

Fix the frame first. The implementation gets easier after that.
